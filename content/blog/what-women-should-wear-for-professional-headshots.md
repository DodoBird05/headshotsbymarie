The question I hear most before a session is simple: what should I wear?

My answer is just as simple. Wear what you would wear to an important business meeting.

That is the same advice I give men, and it holds up for the same reason. The clothes you would choose for a meeting that matters are the clothes that make you look capable, put-together, and like the professional you are. Your headshot needs to do exactly that. So start there.

But there is a little more to it, especially for a tight portrait crop where your outfit sits right next to your face. Here is everything that actually matters.

## Start With One Question: Where Does Your Work Happen?

Before we talk about colors or necklines, answer this. What would you wear on the most important day of your actual job?

For most professionals, the answer is business attire. An <a href="/executive-headshots/" style="color: #333; text-decoration: underline;">executive, an attorney, a consultant, a financial advisor, a realtor</a>: your important-meeting outfit is exactly right, and the rest of this guide is for you.

But for some professions, the answer is different, and that difference matters. We will come back to that at the end. First, the essentials that apply to almost everyone.

## A Blazer Is Your Most Reliable Friend

If you are unsure what to bring, bring a structured blazer or jacket.

A blazer does an enormous amount of work in a headshot. It cleans up the line of your shoulders, frames your neck and face, and adds instant authority and polish. It is the single most dependable piece you can wear, and it photographs beautifully almost every time.

Navy, black, gray, cream: any of these works. The color of the jacket matters less than the structure it provides.

## Add One Pop of Color Under a Neutral

Here is a combination that works nearly every time: a neutral jacket with a colorful top underneath.

Pink under navy. Teal under black. A warm coral under gray. The neutral jacket grounds the image and keeps it professional. The color underneath brings life and warmth to your face, and draws the eye upward to your expression.

This is also where knowing your best colors pays off. A shade that flatters your complexion will make you look healthy and vibrant, while the wrong one can wash you out. If you have never found your <a href="/tips-guides/what-colors-to-wear-for-your-headshot/" style="color: #333; text-decoration: underline;">color season</a>, it is worth a few minutes before your session. The right color does quiet, powerful work in a photograph.

## Choose Solids Over Busy Patterns

In a tight headshot crop, busy patterns compete with your face and pull attention away from where it belongs.

Solid colors, or very subtle patterns, keep the focus on you. Loud prints, tight stripes, and small repeating patterns can also create distracting visual effects on camera. When in doubt, go solid.

## Mind Your Neckline

Necklines that open the frame and draw the eye upward tend to work best. A V-neck, a soft open collar, or a simple scoop all guide attention toward your face.

Very high, closed necklines can sometimes feel heavy in a portrait, and very low ones can pull focus. Somewhere in between, something that feels comfortable and frames your collarbone, is usually ideal.

## Fit Is Everything

This is the detail people underestimate most. Fit reads as polish.

Clothing that fits you cleanly looks professional and intentional. Clothing that is too big looks sloppy in a photo, and clothing that is too tight looks strained. This matters more than how expensive or formal the piece is. A simple, well-fitting top will always outperform a fancier one that does not fit right.

Even a relaxed, casual top works beautifully in a headshot as long as it fits you well.

## A Few Practical Tips

- **Bring options.** Two or three tops and a jacket or two give us room to see what photographs best on you. We can decide together before we shoot.
- **Iron everything.** Wrinkles are distracting and hard to fully fix later. Bring your pieces on hangers if you can.
- **Keep accessories simple.** A delicate necklace or small earrings add polish. Anything large or shiny can pull focus from your face.
- **Think about longevity.** Trendy pieces date a photo quickly. Classic, simple clothing keeps your headshot usable for years.

## Quick Do and Don't

**Do:**

- Do bring options. Two or three tops and a jacket give us room to find what photographs best on you.
- Do iron everything. Wrinkles are distracting and hard to fully fix later.
- Do prioritize fit. A simple, well-fitting piece beats a fancier one that does not fit right, every time.

**Don't:**

- Don't wear puffy sleeves or flowy collars. They add bulk near your face and can overwhelm a tight crop.
- Don't wear spaghetti straps. In a close portrait, thin straps can read as no clothing at all, which is not the look you want for a professional image.
- Don't wear bold patterns. They compete with your face and can create distracting visual effects on camera.

## The Exception: Branding Images

Everything above assumes a classic professional headshot. But if you are creating a <a href="/personal-branding/" style="color: #333; text-decoration: underline;">personal branding image</a>, the goal shifts, and so does the wardrobe.

A branding image has one job: to show who you are and what you do in a single glance. The clothing should do that work instantly, before anyone reads a word about you.

That means a doctor should wear scrubs, not a suit. A hydrogeologist should be in a hard hat and safety vest out in the field. A chef belongs in their whites. A nurse in their uniform. In these cases, the professional attire is the story. Putting that same doctor in a blazer would actually hide what makes them recognizable and relatable.

So the deeper rule underneath all of this is not really about suits or blazers. It is this: dress the way you dress on the most important day of your real work. For most people, that is an important business meeting. For some, it is the uniform that instantly tells the world exactly who they are.

## When You Are Not Sure

If you are ever unsure what to bring, just ask. Talking through wardrobe before a session is one of my favorite parts of the process, and it is far easier to get it right beforehand than to wish we had afterward.

Bring what makes you feel like the most capable, confident version of yourself. Then we will do the rest together.
